{% if contact.SKIN_TYPE == "vata" %}

If you could only adopt one ritual for your Vata skin constitution, Abhyanga (warm oil self-massage) should be it.

Because Vata skin lacks natural oil production and reacts strongly to stress and cold, topical creams often only scratch the surface. Massaging warm, nourishing oil into your skin before a bath creates a protective, insulating shield against the elements while calming the nervous system.

How to Practice Vata Abhyanga:

  1. Warm the Oil: Take organic sesame oil or almond oil and warm it gently in a small bowl of hot water.
  2. Long Strokes: Massage the oil into your body and face using long, sweeping strokes over your limbs and circular motions over your joints.
  3. The Pause: Let the warm oil sink in for 5 to 10 minutes while you sip warm herbal tea.
  4. Rinse: Step into a warm bath or shower (avoid harsh soaps). Pat dry gently, leaving a microscopic layer of moisture locked into your skin.

Your skin will instantly respond with a soft, nourished elasticity that commercial lotions simply cannot replicate.

{% elif contact.SKIN_TYPE == "pitta" %}

Pitta skin often shies away from facial oils out of fear of breakouts—yet skipping nourishment leaves the skin barrier vulnerable and easily irritated. The secret lies in choosing cooling, lightweight oils and pairing them with calming floral hydrosols.

How to Practice the Pitta Evening Cool-Down Ritual:

  1. Mist First: Before applying anything, mist your clean face generously with pure, steam-distilled edible rosewater (Gulab Jal) to immediately lower skin surface temperature.
  2. Cooling Oil Application: Warm just 2–3 drops of cold-pressed coconut oil or sunflower oil between your fingertips (avoid warming sesame or mustard oils, which hold too much heat for Pitta).
  3. Gentle Touch: Press—don't rub—the oil gently into damp skin using upward, featherlight strokes. Over-massaging generates friction and heat; Pitta skin responds best to a soft, unhurried touch.
  4. Nighttime Reset: Rinse with cool or lukewarm water—never hot—to protect delicate capillaries and preserve your natural lipid barrier.
{% elif contact.SKIN_TYPE == "kapha" %}

Because Kapha skin tends to retain fluid and hold onto dead skin cells, standard moisturizing lotions and heavy oils will only weigh your skin down. Your skin responds best to dry, stimulating exfoliation.

In Ayurveda, the premier ritual for Kapha imbalance is Udwartana—an invigorating massage using dry herbal powders rather than oil.

How to Practice Kapha Herbal Scrubbing:

  1. The Blend: Mix chickpea flour (besan) or masoor dal flour with a pinch of turmeric and dry ginger powder.
  2. Firm Upward Strokes: Apply the dry or lightly dampened powder to your skin, massaging firmly in upward strokes against the direction of hair growth. This creates friction that warms the tissue, breaks down superficial fat deposits, and opens blocked pores.
  3. Rinse & Stimulate: Rinse off with warm water to leave your skin feeling remarkably light, smooth, and deeply energized.
{% elif contact.SKIN_TYPE == "vata-pitta" %}

Vata-Pitta skin cannot tolerate aggressive scrubbing, hot water, or heavy warming oils like mustard or raw sesame (which can feel too heating for Pitta). Instead, your skin flourishes with liquid hydration sealed under a cooling, nutrient-rich oil.

How to Practice the Vata-Pitta Barrier Ritual:

  1. Drench with Floral Water: Mist clean, slightly damp skin generously with pure steam-distilled rosewater or vetiver (khus) hydrosol to flood thirsty Vata cells with cooling moisture.
  2. Seal with Sweet Almond or Coconut-Jojoba: Warm 3–4 drops of cold-pressed sweet almond oil (rich in soothing vitamin E) between your palms. In warmer months, blend with a drop of fractionated coconut oil.
  3. Slow, Press-and-Release Touch: Gently press your palms onto your cheeks, forehead, and neck—holding for a breath at each spot to calm facial tension without creating friction.
  4. Lukewarm Rinse: Always cleanse with lukewarm or cool water to keep capillaries calm and intact.
{% elif contact.SKIN_TYPE == "vata-kapha" %}

Caring for Vata-Kapha skin requires a balanced hand: your T-zone craves stimulation and gentle exfoliation, while your drier areas need comforting warmth and moisture.

How to Practice the Dual-Action Vata-Kapha Ritual:

  1. Stimulate the Congested Zones: Once a week, use a gentle herbal dry powder (like chickpea flour with a pinch of dry ginger) on your T-zone or areas prone to sluggishness to encourage lymphatic drainage.
  2. Nourish the Dry Zones: Warm a few drops of light sesame or almond oil and press gently into dry cheeks and neck areas.
  3. Lukewarm Rinses: Always cleanse with lukewarm water—never freezing cold or scalding hot—to protect capillaries and balance both elements simultaneously.
{% elif contact.SKIN_TYPE == "pitta-kapha" %}

Because Pitta-Kapha skin battles both oil congestion and inflammatory sensitivity, your cleansing ritual must be both clarifying and non-irritating. Heavy oils will clog pores, while harsh scrubs will anger Pitta capillaries.

How to Practice the Cooling Purification Ritual:

  1. The Cooling Herbal Blend: Mix chickpea flour (besan) or neem leaf powder with a pinch of cooling sandalwood powder and rose petal powder.
  2. Gentle Upward Sweep: Mix into a smooth paste with rosewater and apply to oily, congested zones, massaging very gently in upward strokes to stimulate lymphatic drainage without friction-induced heat.
  3. Cool Water Rinse: Always rinse thoroughly with cool water to soothe inflamed capillaries and instantly close pores.
{% elif contact.SKIN_TYPE == "tridoshic" %}

Because your skin is naturally balanced, your daily care routine should focus on preserving your lipid barrier and nurturing your nervous system.

How to Practice the Tridoshik Maintenance Ritual:

  1. Adaptive Oil Selection: Use light, balancing oils like sesame oil in cooler months, coconut or sunflower oil in peak summer, and almond oil during transitional seasons.
  2. Mindful Touch: Massage your skin using moderate, circular strokes over joints and long strokes over limbs to stimulate healthy lymphatic flow without overwhelming your system.
  3. Hydration Seal: Finish with a light mist of pure rosewater to refresh your complexion and keep your natural glow alive.
